## Chassis and bodywork

- **REWORKED THE WEIGHT DISTRIBUTION BETWEEN THE CHASSIS, BODY PANELS AND POWERTRAIN**
  - Previously, too much of the difference between variants was being covered by engine and chassis node weights. Let's say that, WRC17 has a weight limit of 1190kg while Rally2 has 1230kg, results in 40kg of difference. I actually heavily doubt that they come from engine and chassis due to the reinforcements and weld points, but this was the method before.
  - After inspecting a homologation file of Polo GTI R5, I've realized that in WRC regulations, you could use more aggressive composite materials as panels, while Rally2 both regulations and cost cap can't make this true, so most body panels like doors, hood, tailgate are mostly using steel which are heavier.
  - To compensate, the Rally2 engine assembly is lighter and the WRC17 engine assembly is slightly heavier than in 26.08.18, reducing the large artificial difference between them. Also, the chassis weight difference is much smaller than before.
  - After reading a technical sheet of Xtrac R5 gearbox including front transaxle and rear diff, I decided to add more weight in the transmission. To compensate weight this, I reduced the weight of the central and rear underguards. Some of their previous mass was excessive when considered alongside the structures already represented by neighbouring nodes.
  - There is an important limitation when comparing this with real component weights: one BeamNG node can support an engine mount, a transmission attachment and part of a chassis structure at the same time. These are assembly budgets, not a claim that every node group is an isolated component on a weighing scale.

- **MOVED THE ROLLCAGE INTO CHASSIS MODIFICATION**
  - The cage's nodes, beams and flexbodies now belong to the chassis modification, instead of being a separate removable cage part.
  - After reading an interview of Polo GTI R5, I've found that IRL Rally2 car needs to have a heavier rollcage compared to WRC due to cost and regulations, so I added a heavier Rally2 cage weighs about 47.8 kg.

- Added steel door, hood and tailgate variants
- Adjusted seat and fuel-tank node positions, **The oocupant feature in 0.40 is not implemented due to various weight and COG concerns, needs evaluation**
- Reworked chassis, subframe and powertrain mounting connections
- Adjusted the Rally2 rear wing structure and positioning 40 mm farther forward, adjusted wing-tailgate node weights
- Massively reduced drag on various body parts, now the lift to drag ratio is largely increased
- Updated bodywork flexbody assignments, material references and some collision / aerodynamic triangles alongside the structural changes.

## Engine and transmission (THIS IS HUGE!)
- Slightly reduced engine torque in the redline range.
- Reduced engine block beam stiffness fore more stability.
- Rebalanced engine, gearbox, transfer-case and rear differential node weights, and revised their supporting beams and mounts.
- Rebalanced engine / exhaust gains and gear-whine levels due to changes in 0.40 update.
- Moved throttle-map calibration into the individual ECU data, so each engine specification can have its own curves.
- Significantly reduced the aggressiveness of default throttle map, now all **Subtle Linear** maps are literally more linear.
- **FINAL DRIVE AND GEARBOX REWORK**
  - After various researching considering calculations around the center axle, I've decided adjust the powertrain layout.
  - Before, all reduction happens in the front and rear differential, this is similar to Subaru WRX ARA but not common in Rally2 cars. From a 2020 FIA file regarding some informations about Rally1 hybrid system, it shows the usual gear ratio in the rear differntial of a WRC car is at 2.5~3.5, which after research, **IS NOT THE ACTUAL FINAL DRIVE**.
  - Reference to multiple transverse Rally2 cars, now the main reduction is applied inside the transfer-case powertrain device. The front differential runs at **1:1**, the main final-drive selection is now shared between gearboxes.
  - Standardized gear-set names around tooth counts, including **15x62** for the Rally2-related main final drive and **15x67** for WRC17. ~~RX retains its own final-drive selection.~~
  - The AWD rear output uses a **34x13 output gear set** and a **13x34 rear gear ratio**. Their reciprocal ratios cancel, keeping the front and rear axle speeds matched while representing the separate rear-drive gearing in the range of 2.5~3.5, which is confirmed the exactly same struture on i20N Rally2 and Polo GTI R5.
  - WRC17's final drive is slightly shorter, reducing top-speed from roughly 193–194 km/h to 191 km/h (measured with Type-A, tarmac setup).

- **COMPLETE REVAMP OF TORQUE NUMBERS DUE TO ABOVE FINAL DRIVE CHANGES**
  - Due to front axle have 1:1 RPM from the transfercase, the torsion reactor and center locking torque now has a much higher torque reading
  - Higher chassis flex due to much higher torsionReactor reading is seen at the front wheels under power, braking or shifting. Increasing subframe torque mounting stiffness alone did not remove all of it

- Increased the locked transfer case's locking capacity from 2500 to 4000 Nm

- **REBUILT THE ACTIVE CENTER DIFFERENTIAL'S MAP CALCULATION**
  - Also from the observations above, I've realized some serious mistakes in the torque numbers of Active Center Differential. Before, I was not passing final drive values into calculations, despite having a component that stores this value. I was giving the values ideally at wheel axle, like **100~200Nm**, where this should be multiplied by the final drive ratios into the center differential. For example, my final drive was at about **4.47**, so the value should be **447~894Nm, if OG is 100~200Nm**. This means the locking torque was about 4.5 times smaller than it should be, meaning the braking locking power is much less than expected
  - After above final drive translation, the numbers will be again multipled by the final drives, to get 2000~4000Nm level of torque. This matches the center locking splitshaft's torque, **also matches RBR NGP's default 3000~4000Nm**
  - The small base preload used by coast and throttle map is now **80 Nm**, which translate to about **18Nm** at wheel axle
  - The braking preload is now a **15x–60x multiplier** instead of a fixed torque number, the base is the same above, so braking locking torque is working more like a preload amplifier in the high input ranges, where low input ranges considering about 45Nm of engine braking, it still translates to hundreds of Nm of torque. **Which essentially means the braking map is like a mixer of sources, in different input and gear ratios, both sources provide a different mix of locking torque**. By default, the braking preload contribution can reach **3200 Nm**
  - Replaced independently adjustable pedal start / maximum points with **start + travel through the remaining pedal range**
  - Reorganized the steering controls as **Steering Lock Cut** and **Speed Map Factor**, increasing both sliders result in higher lock
  - Switched Lua algorithm to **map weights** instead of map activations. This brings a **20 ms smooth transition** between coast, throttle and braking maps, rather than instantly switching between the small preload and the braking capacity
  - Updated controller variable namings to match their Jbeam namings
  - Removed the coast-threshold tuning slider and fixed the threshold at 5%
  - Fixed the repeated steering reduction in the braking calculation

- **RECALIBRATED MECHANICAL LSDs**
  - Removed the friction-face multiplier from preload.
  - Updated ramp calculation formulas. This perfectly matches the data from different providers and is mathematically correct. **Just to note that before it was correct as well, but too complex in the formula, and according to real data, the locking becomes 0% at 85 instead of 90 degrees.**
  - The front drive-ramp choices are now 46 / 50 / 62 degrees, each with 70 / 80-degree coast options.
  - The rear choices are now 40 / 46 / 54 degrees, with 80-degree coast.
  - The differential workbook and in-game parts now use the same curve. Its displayed locking percentage consistently follows the project's **2 × lsdLockCoef** convention.

- The mechanical center differential uses the same revised calculation including preload and ramp angles.
- Reduced clutch lock-spring stiffness from **0.365 to 0.28** to reduce the sharp load transfer during gear changes, particularly braking downshifts.
- Retuned transmission friction values.
- Newly added **multiShiftLights.lua** based on vanilla shiftLights.lua now accepts different flashing RPM on different gears. The thresholds are supplied by the ECU, and the old shift-light tuning sliders have been removed.
- Updated shift light RPMs on different cars.
- Added a separate Rally1 ECU settings entry so its five-speed setup does not have to share WRC17's six-speed light settings.
- Hid the experimental engine-map / ALS tuning controls for now. Further eWastegate, ALS and launch-control development is reserved for a later update.

## Suspension (THIS IS EVEN HUGER!)

- **NEW MESH FOR ALL WHEELS**
  - Tarmac wheels are 15-spoke, gravel wheels are 11-spoke as the IRL wheels are.
  - The meshes are created from 0 so the topology is much cleaner.
  - Color scheme is separated individually so every car can have every wheel it wants
  - Tyre sizes are referenced from IRL ACB11, Pirelli Scorpion and P Zero, where I've found that **the 235/205 doesn't actually has the same size as it's label**
  - Recreated gravel tyre mesh to a more symmetrical shape
  - Separated wheel / rim parts from tyre definitions and reorganized the associated hub, flexbody and slot data. Updated rim choices and naming which fits in BeamNG's wheel size selector/

- Adjusted rally tyre load sensitivity, tread, sliding behaviour and sidewall support, and updated some tyre-width / sidewall parameters.

- **REDUCED UNSPRUNG MASS AND REWORKED THE SUPPORTING STRUCTURE**
  - After countless observations, I've now concluded a rule/baseline. **The suspension parts aren't as super stiff/strong as I believe, and compromises IRL for lightweight do exist**.
  - Firstly, the **Subframe is very light IRL**. Multiple sources claiming subframe has a top weight limit, and homologation from Polo GTI R5 and parts from C3 Rally2 all shows the subframe is around **12kg**. Obviously, due to above node weight allocation limits and BeamNG's physics, I can't just say fx1+fx2+fx3+fx4 total at 12kg while retaining rigidity.
  - After test and measurements, now fx1 and fx2 nodes are around 4.5kg, fx3 and fx4 nodes are hugely reduced. fx1 and fx2 nodes are combined with control arms, fx3 and fx4 nodes are combined with chassis. At the same time, the **subframe stiffness is about 16~33% lower which should not have any meaningful flex, meaning the OG beams have enough headroom to drop**.
  - Same things goes for unsprung mass, hugely reduced weight in the upright / control-arm / steering connection nodes, damper lower ends and gravel brake nodes. Tarmac brake node weight is added due to larger disc.
  - Revised control-arm beams, camber links, rails, steering connections and subframe mounts to suit the lighter structure. **Even after moderate beam stiffness drop, this surprisingly somehow made the assembly more stable**.
  - After calculation by Codex, the structural damping beam are relocated so the stability damping beams has **MUCH** less affect on damper movements.
  - In driving tests, the lighter suspension responded more quickly and felt more natural over the surface. It also exposed sharper steering feedback and some small unloaded-wheel movements that had previously been suppressed by the heavier assemblies.

- Added **Low / High control-arm mounting points**. The front adjustment changes the anti-dive geometry; the rear adjustment provides a suspension-geometry option for tarmac.
- Reorganized these controls with camber, toe and caster under **Alignment**. Removed track-width tuning.
- Fixed the steering-rack height and replaced the rack-shim adjustment with a front **Bumpsteer Spacer**, acting at the outer steering connection like the rear adjustment.

- **ONE OF THE BIGGEST FINDINGS: SOME OF THE SUSPENSION'S RESISTANCE WAS COMING FROM THE WRONG SIDE OF THE STOPS**
  - The old mechanical rebound-stop setup could add substantial damping while the suspension was compressing. Likewise, the mechanical bump-stop setup could resist the suspension as it extended away from compression.
  - The ideal behind this, was setting the material property of the mechanical stops, where they have some damping when extending. **However, IRL, the stops do not stick to the piston when extending, after hitting the stops, when the suspension moves away from the stops, although the stops are probably extending with the damping on their own, it does not affect to the piston itself so no such damping should be presented in the Jbeam**.
  - This was especially noticeable around the ends of travel. Some of the twisting or hesitation had led to heavier nodes and stiffer structure being used to contain it.
  - Removed the excessive opposite-direction damping and standardized those small background values at **100 N·s/m**, while retaining the intended end-stop action.
  - Mechanical rebound-stop stiffness is now **654,114 N/m at the front** and **400,100 N/m at the rear**. The rear was softened to allow a more comparable amount of elastic extension at the end of travel, now both front and rear will have about 4~5mm of compressing travel.
  - Mechanical bump stops are calibrated by surface and specification. Gravel retains its own settings; ordinary tarmac uses **651,000 N/m front / 551,000 N/m rear**, while Rally2 tarmac retains its separate **675,000 / 565,000 N/m** adjustable setup.
  - On the bump side, actually due to a progressive curve, the stiffness is not changed by tuning, rather than the boundZone which controls the travel. Corrected the trigger points and bound zones to follow the intended mechanical stop travel, including Rally2's adjustable tarmac stops.
  - WRC17, Rally1 tarmac and RX mechanical bump-stop length is now 17 mm.
  - Reduced hydraulic rebound-stop damping to gravel 2000 N·s/m, tarmac 3000 N·s/m.
  - Standardized the small spring terms in all hydraulic bump and rebound stops at **100 N/m** and corrected left / right mismatches.
  - This made it possible to reconsider the suspension mass and stiffness on their own merits, rather than keeping excess mass to hide an unwanted damping effect.

- **REWORKED THE DAMPER CURVES, ADJUSTERS AND MODEL RANGE**
  - From a lore view, the dampers now have silimlar levels to real life brands. The series will be 3, 4, 6, 8, while only 6 and 8 is used in the mod.
  - Renamed the range from MRA / MRX to **RA**: RA60 / RA65 for Rally2 tarmac / gravel, RA80 / RA85 for WRC17, RA80/2 / RA85/2 for Rally1, and RA88 / RA68 for RX1 / RX3.
  - Significant Jbeam file structure has been changed, now the damper has different valving slots, in the Jbeam it only passes variables from the upper layer and all calculations and values are stored in the components section, so the final Jbeam looks **MUCH** cleaner.
  - After some reasearching and reasoning, LS and HS clicks are doing entirely different things than before, and it should be much more realistic. From below, low-speed compression is called LSC, or c; high-speed compression is called HSC, or C; rebound is called r.
  - Type-A and Type-B are 2 different style valvings. Type-A has a 3-stage curve. At low speed, the damping rises fast, after reaching it's **first knee point**, the preload valve is opened by fluid, where we have our first small slope drop in the curve. At the **second knee point**, this is where the shim stacks have their effects, the curve becomes more progressive as the piston speed rises. Type-B is mostly digressive after first knee, however in some specific setups the high speed can still gain back a bit of slope due to shim stacks.
  - LSC changes the slope without moving the knee points. Type-A's HSC acts on the second knee and slope after; Type-B's acts on the first knee. **Damper workbooks are built and you can see the curves with a calculator**.
  - Retuned the size of the click changes. Gravel, tarmac and RX have their own working ranges rather than sharing a curve only rescaled by name.
  - The 6-series uses **10 HSC**, the 8-series uses **20 HSC**, retains adjustable hydraulic bump-stop damping.
  - Removed adjustable HRS.
  - Removed HRS from **RA60, RA65 and RA80/2**.
  - RA88 is **3+1-Way**, while RA68 uses the 6-series arrangement.
  - Rebound click counts remain model-specific, including RA80 and 85 have 60 clicks and other models have 40 clicks.
  - **Removed the separate LRS multiplier slider** and organized the blocker under the selected valving.
  - Removed the gravel WRC17 DSV setup so when implemented, DSV is RA80 only.

- **LRS NOW READS THE DAMPER THAT IS ACTUALLY INSTALLED**
  - Removed the duplicate damping-data tables in Jbeam used to describe the same damper again for Lua. The controller builds its damping data from the named physical damper beams during initialization.
  - The additional HS beam is included when reporting the full compression curve, and the two transition velocities are treated as velocity 1 / velocity 2 rather than assuming that every valve design has the same “HS knee point”.
  - LRS multiplier now belongs to the selected valving. Type-A and Type-B can use different rebound-relief factors on gravel, tarmac and RX.
  - **While LRS multiplier slider has been removed**, it now also follows the rebound adjuster through a ±0.05 range: softer rebound uses less relief, while firmer rebound allows more relief when LRS opens.
  - Retuned the trigger thresholds.
  - Added selectable per-wheel live debugging for opening / closing behaviour.

- **REBUILT THE ANTI-ROLL BARS AROUND ADJUSTABLE CANTILEVERS**
  - Before, I was thinking most WRC cars use the mounting position changes on the control arm, to control and motion ratio, to control the effectiveness. In my car's specific geometry, it was strangly positioned and not very effective.
  - After some research, indeed there are methods that uses holes on teh cantilevers to change the effective lever legnth, thus different ARB stiffness which I originally wanted. Polo GTI R5's rear, **GR Yaris Rally1's rear**, and all kinds of different cars such as Subaru's ARA car and TCR cars are all using the method.
  - So I'm happy to report that I replaced the old adjustment at the lower control-arm pickup with three effective cantilever-length positions: **L / M / H**, and it's very effective, giving much more useful separation!
  - Added **solid and lightweight cantilever parts**. This is also due to Rally2 cost and regulations, IRL they all use solid while WRC can use lightweight ones. Their nodes and beams belong to those parts, so selecting a different cantilever also changes the corresponding weight.
  - Corresponding meshes are recreated from 0, and using props system for the cantilever, the ARB point selection can also matches on the mesh!
  - **Speaking of actual stiffness:**
  -  I was measuring the z axis movement of the cantilever tip and using that to compare the bars, that works only under restricted geometry assumptions.
  - A tip moving farther in Z does not necessarily mean the bar has twisted farther. Different radius and starting angles can change the vertical displacement even when the actual rotation is the same.
  - The new workbook calculates angular movement around the bar's axis from the node positions, then compares it with wheel travel. This also accounts for the fact that the two reference nodes do not necessarily share the same X coordinate.
  - Measurements start from the **settled, fully extended position after precompression**, using the wheel jack debug part. The original spawn coordinates are not a reliable start point for every cantilever setting. It ends at bumpstops where springs are removed. The resulting equivalent wheel rate follows **torsional stiffness × angular motion ratio squared**, with the appropriate unit conversion.
  - The figures are an equivalent rate over the measured travel interval, as comparing to inaccurate and ineffective old methods, the wheel stiffness is **MORE THAN TWICE** and significantly reduced tarmac body roll.
