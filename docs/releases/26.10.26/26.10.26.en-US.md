# Qlourie Astral R — 26.10.26

**English** | [简体中文](./26.10.26.zh-CN.md)

> Draft compared against release **26.08.18**, through **b949e66**. The unfinished items at the end are placeholders for this release, not completed features. Model screenshots will be added separately.

**THIS IS A LARGE PHYSICS UPDATE.** A lot has changed underneath the car: where its weight is carried, how the suspension moves, how the anti-roll bars work, and how torque travels through the drivetrain. There are also new damper calibrations, revised setups, and English / Simplified Chinese localization throughout the parts and tuning menus.

**OLD CUSTOM CONFIGURATIONS WILL NEED CHECKING.** Several part names, slots and tuning variables have changed. Please start with the updated supplied configurations when comparing the handling; an old saved setup may retain obsolete values or load different defaults.

## Chassis and bodywork

- **REWORKED THE WEIGHT DISTRIBUTION BETWEEN THE CHASSIS, BODY PANELS AND POWERTRAIN**
  - Previously, too much of the difference between variants was being covered by engine and chassis node weights. This update goes through the individual assemblies instead: engine, gearbox, differential, guards, cage, body panels and suspension.
  - The Rally2 engine assembly is lighter and the WRC17 engine assembly is slightly heavier than in 26.08.18, reducing the large artificial difference between them. Gearbox and rear differential weights have also been redistributed to their own nodes.
  - Reduced the weight of the central and rear underguards. Some of their previous mass was excessive when considered alongside the structures already represented by neighbouring nodes.
  - Rebalanced the front, floor and rear chassis weights. The compensation belongs to the chassis modification itself, so fitting gravel or tarmac suspension does not secretly change the weight of the same chassis.
  - The main Rally2 and WRC17 running-weight targets remain approximately **1420 kg and 1390 kg** respectively. This is a redistribution of mass, rather than simply making every variant lighter.
  - There is an important limitation when comparing this with real component weights: one BeamNG node can support an engine mount, a transmission attachment and part of a chassis structure at the same time. These are assembly budgets, not a claim that every node group is an isolated component on a weighing scale.

- **MOVED THE ROLLCAGE INTO CHASSIS MODIFICATION**
  - The cage's nodes, beams and flexbodies now belong to the chassis modification, instead of being a separate removable cage part.
  - Added a heavier Rally2 cage specification. Its cage nodes total about **47.8 kg**, compared with about **40.1 kg** for the current WRC17 specification.
  - This gives the two chassis specifications a more deliberate structural and weight difference. The cage remains part of the welded structure of the car.

- Added **steel door and tailgate variants**, with their weight distributed over the panel, hinge and rigidifier nodes. The existing lighter panels remain available for the corresponding specifications.
- Increased the weight of the **existing Rally2 hood**, rather than adding another duplicate hood part.
- Adjusted seat, occupant-reference and fuel-tank node positions, together with the surrounding attachments and weight allocation.
- Reworked chassis, subframe and powertrain mounting connections as part of the weight changes. Some connections are stronger, while others have less stiffness or damping where the old values were masking a different problem.

- Adjusted the **Rally2 rear wing structure and positioning**. Relative to 26.08.18, the main wing / endplate / swan-neck node assembly is approximately **40 mm farther forward**; the body mounting points remain in place.
- Removed the Rally2 wing's duplicate tailgate-node weight overrides, so those nodes are owned by the tailgate again.
- Updated bodywork flexbody assignments, material references and some collision / aerodynamic triangles alongside the structural changes.

> **SCREENSHOTS TO ADD:** body panel and wing changes; any additional model / topology changes to show from Blender or the game.

- Added separate **Rally2 WRC Kit** front bumper, front fenders, lip and rear wing parts. The wing uses the endplate, high wing and fin arrangement, while shared lights, badges and other small parts retain their existing meshes.
- Added the corresponding prototype configuration with a **33 mm restrictor**. It remains **hidden from the normal configuration list** while its aerodynamics, weight and final specification are being developed.

## Engine and transmission

- **REWORKED THE FINAL-DRIVE AND TRANSFER-CASE ARRANGEMENT**
  - The main final-drive selection is now shared between the five- and six-speed transmissions, instead of being tied to separate front differential variants.
  - The main reduction is applied inside the transfer-case powertrain device. The front differential runs at **1:1**.
  - The AWD rear output uses a **34x13 output gear set** and a **13x34 rear gear ratio**. Their reciprocal ratios cancel, keeping the front and rear axle speeds matched while representing the separate rear-drive gearing.
  - The output gear set is visible as a transfer-case part. It does not require another extra shaft in the powertrain diagram.
  - Standardized gear-set names around tooth counts, including **15x62** for the Rally2-related main final drive and **15x67** for WRC17. RX retains its own final-drive selection.
  - Rescaled the affected gearbox ratio sets and stored the resulting ratios directly, to three decimal places. The consolidation preserves the intended overall gearing, apart from deliberate ratio changes.
  - WRC17's final drive is slightly shorter, reducing its top-speed target from the previous roughly 193–194 km/h tarmac setup towards 191 km/h. The final measured speed still depends on the configuration and tyres.

- **THE LOCATION OF A GEAR REDUCTION MATTERS, EVEN WHEN THE WHEEL SPEED STAYS THE SAME**
  - Two powertrain layouts can produce the same engine RPM and road speed, but have very different torque and speed at the center coupling or a torsion reactor.
  - This was especially important when comparing locking-torque settings. A number in Nm belongs to the shaft or device where it is applied; it cannot be compared with another car's number without also checking the reduction ratios around it.
  - The current center-differential settings use torque values at their actual modeled location. There is no additional final-drive multiplier hidden in the controller's preload calculation.
  - Moving the main reduction also changed the torque seen by the front torsion reactor. Some of the movement seen at the front wheels under power, braking or shifting comes from this load path through the engine mounts and subframe.
  - That is different from measuring bump steer by compressing an unpowered suspension. Increasing one subframe mounting stiffness alone did not remove all of it.
  - The current front reaction arrangement is retained for this release. The work here establishes a more consistent drivetrain layout; it does not claim that all power-related toe movement has disappeared.

- **REBUILT THE ACTIVE CENTER DIFFERENTIAL'S MAP CALCULATION**
  - Separated the small throttle / coast preload from the much larger braking capacity. The small base preload is now **80 Nm**; the braking adjustment is a **15x–60x multiplier**, with a part default of **40x**.
  - At the default multiplier, the braking preload contribution can reach **3200 Nm** before steering, left-foot braking and handbrake corrections. This is available locking capacity, not a constant torque forced through the drivetrain.
  - The input-torque-dependent LSD contribution is retained alongside that preload contribution.
  - Fixed the repeated steering reduction in the braking calculation. Steering was already included in the pedal-map gain, but the braking preload was then reduced by steering a second time.
  - The minimum lock setting belongs to the pedal map. Steering is allowed to release the result below that minimum, all the way to zero; the final clamp no longer raises it back to the pedal-map minimum.
  - Replaced independently adjustable pedal start / maximum points with **start + travel through the remaining pedal range**. The maximum point can no longer end up below the start point. The part defaults still reach full throttle-map gain at about **90% throttle** and full brake-map gain at about **75% brake**.
  - Reorganized the steering controls as **Steering Lock Cut** and **Speed Map Factor**. Increasing Steering Lock Cut reduces steering's unlocking effect. Increasing Speed Map Factor reduces that steering release more strongly as speed rises.
  - Left-foot braking remains a modifier of each candidate pedal map. It is applied **before** the maps are blended together.
  - Added a **20 ms smooth transition** between coast, throttle and braking maps, rather than instantly switching between the small preload and the braking capacity. A new transition begins from the current mixture if the driver's inputs change again partway through it.
  - Handbrake release is applied after the map calculation and blending. The existing rear-drive disconnection behaviour is retained.
  - The controller now updates in the fixed-step loop, and its JBeam input names match the names used in Lua.
  - Removed the coast-threshold tuning slider and fixed the threshold at **5%**. WRC17 configurations have been updated to the new map variables, while retaining their intended front / rear torque split.

- Increased the Rally2-style locked transfer case's finite locking capacity from **2500 to 4000 Nm**.
  - A locked split shaft in the simulation still has a torque capacity; its name does not make it an infinitely rigid connection.
  - During testing, a wheel-speed difference that initially looked like a complete axle disconnect turned out to involve a single wheel. Front / rear coupling behaviour and left / right differential behaviour need to be checked separately.

- **RECALIBRATED THE FRONT, REAR AND MECHANICAL CENTER LSDs**
  - Removed the friction-face multiplier from preload. Selecting **60 Nm now means 60 Nm of preload**, regardless of the number of active friction faces.
  - Replaced the old ramp normalization with a revised curve using a **5-degree effective offset** and proportional friction-face scaling. This better follows the manual data used for the calibration, including the ramp-dependent contribution approaching zero at 85 degrees.
  - This is a fitted model for the available reference data, rather than a claim that all real differentials follow one exact ramp formula.
  - The front drive-ramp choices are now **46 / 50 / 62 degrees**, each with **70 / 80-degree coast** options.
  - The rear choices are now **40 / 46 / 54 degrees**, with **80-degree coast**. The spacing was chosen to avoid making different ramp / friction-face combinations unnecessarily similar.
  - The mechanical center differential uses the same revised calculation, retaining its **45-degree drive** and **30 / 50-degree coast** choices.
  - The differential workbook and in-game parts now use the same curve. Its displayed locking percentage consistently follows the project's **2 × lsdLockCoef** convention.

- Rebalanced engine, gearbox, transfer-case and rear differential node weights, and revised their supporting beams and mounts.
- Reduced clutch lock-spring stiffness from **0.365 to 0.28** to reduce the sharp load transfer during gear changes, particularly braking downshifts.
- Retuned transmission friction and sound settings. The final-drive-dependent friction compensation used during development has been removed.
- Rebalanced engine / exhaust gains and gear-whine levels for the newer BeamNG sound behaviour, including the very prominent upper-gear whine under load.

- Moved throttle-map calibration into the individual ECU data, so each engine specification can have its own curves. Fixed the zero-curvature case to produce a linear response.
- Replaced the old shift-light setup with a controller that supports **a different shift RPM for every gear**. The thresholds are supplied by the ECU, and the old shift-light tuning sliders have been removed.
- Added a separate Rally1 ECU settings entry so its five-speed setup does not have to share WRC17's six-speed light settings.
- Hid the experimental engine-map / ALS tuning controls for now. Further eWastegate, ALS and launch-control development is reserved for a later update.

> **TO FINISH BEFORE RELEASE:** calculate and test the individual shift-light RPMs from each engine's output curve and gearbox ratios. The controller supports them, but the current thresholds are still uniform across the forward gears of each specification.

## Suspension

- **REDUCED UNSPRUNG MASS AND REWORKED THE SUPPORTING STRUCTURE**
  - Reduced weight in the upright / control-arm / steering connection nodes, damper lower ends and gravel brake nodes, instead of relying on heavy assemblies to suppress movement.
  - For example, the main outer suspension nodes are now around **6.5 kg instead of 8.5 kg**, while the lower damper nodes are approximately **3.3 kg on gravel and 3.0 kg on tarmac**.
  - Tarmac brake nodes retain more weight than gravel ones to represent the larger brake assembly. These nodes are not treated as a bare brake disc alone.
  - Revised control-arm beams, camber links, rails, steering connections and subframe mounts to suit the lighter structure. Front / rear camber-link stiffness is now **6,501,000 / 4,501,000 N/m**.
  - Model-specific upper strut mounting weights are retained where they differ. This is not a blanket copy of one suspension's entire mass distribution onto every variant.
  - The changes were developed on Rally2 and carried across to WRC17, Rally1, RX1 and RX3, with their road-surface and model differences retained.
  - In driving tests, the lighter Rally2 suspension responded more quickly and felt more natural over the surface. It also exposed sharper steering feedback and some small unloaded-wheel movements that had previously been suppressed by the heavier assemblies.

- **ONE OF THE BIGGEST FINDINGS: SOME OF THE SUSPENSION'S RESISTANCE WAS COMING FROM THE WRONG SIDE OF THE STOPS**
  - The old mechanical rebound-stop setup could add substantial damping while the suspension was compressing. Likewise, the mechanical bump-stop setup could resist the suspension as it extended away from compression.
  - This was especially noticeable around the ends of travel. Some of the twisting or hesitation had led to heavier nodes and stiffer structure being used to contain it.
  - Removed the excessive opposite-direction damping and standardized those small background values at **100 N·s/m**, while retaining the intended end-stop action.
  - Mechanical rebound-stop stiffness is now **654,114 N/m at the front** and **400,100 N/m at the rear**. The rear was softened to allow a more comparable amount of elastic extension at the end of travel.
  - Reworked hydraulic rebound-stop damping and travel. Where fitted, the gravel calibration uses **20 mm / 2000 N·s/m**, and the tarmac calibration uses **15 mm / 3000 N·s/m**.
  - Standardized the small spring terms in all hydraulic bump and rebound stops at **100 N/m** and corrected left / right mismatches.
  - Mechanical bump stops are calibrated by surface and specification. Gravel retains its own settings; ordinary tarmac uses **651,000 N/m front / 551,000 N/m rear**, while Rally2 tarmac retains its separate **675,000 / 565,000 N/m** adjustable setup.
  - WRC17 and Rally1 tarmac mechanical bump-stop travel is now **17 mm**. RX3 also uses 17 mm; RX1 retains 10 mm with the ordinary tarmac spring-rate values.
  - Corrected the trigger points and bound zones to follow the intended mechanical stop travel, including Rally2's adjustable tarmac stops.
  - This made it possible to reconsider the suspension mass and stiffness on their own merits, rather than keeping excess mass to hide an unwanted damping effect.

- **REWORKED THE DAMPER CURVES, ADJUSTERS AND MODEL RANGE**
  - Renamed the range from MRA / MRX to **RA**: RA60 / RA65 for Rally2 tarmac / gravel, RA80 / RA85 for WRC17, RA80/2 / RA85/2 for Rally1, and RA88 / RA68 for RX1 / RX3.
  - Rebuilt the gravel and tarmac valving layouts around the same beam structure, with separate front / rear **Type-A and Type-B** calibration parts. RX now has its own corresponding valving family.
  - Low-speed compression changes the slope without moving the knee points. Type-A's high-speed adjustment acts on the second knee; Type-B's acts on the first knee.
  - Revised the compression curve progression so firmer bump settings can become more digressive through the relevant transition, instead of automatically becoming both steeper and less digressive.
  - Retuned the size of the click changes. Gravel, tarmac and RX have their own working ranges rather than sharing a curve only rescaled by name.
  - The tarmac Type-B calibration uses zero damping in the extra high-speed beam, keeping the common physical structure without adding an unwanted extra stage.
  - The 6-series uses **10 high-speed compression clicks**. Its normal adjusters are low-speed compression, high-speed compression and rebound; hydraulic bump-stop damping is fixed where that system is fitted.
  - The 8-series retains adjustable hydraulic bump-stop damping. Hydraulic rebound stops, where fitted, use fixed calibration rather than another tuning slider.
  - Removed HRS from **RA60, RA65 and RA80/2**. RX feature allocation was also revised: RA88 is **3+1-Way**, while RA68 uses the 6-series arrangement.
  - Rebound click counts remain model-specific, including WRC17's 60-click range and the Rally1 / RX 40-click ranges.
  - Removed the separate LRS multiplier slider and organized the blocker under the selected valving. Damper adjustment defaults now sit in the middle of their ranges.
  - Removed the gravel WRC17 DSV setup. The intended feature split keeps DSV with the WRC17 tarmac specification; the remaining Rally1 feature audit is listed below.

- **LRS NOW READS THE DAMPER THAT IS ACTUALLY INSTALLED**
  - Removed the duplicate damping-data tables used to describe the same damper again for Lua. The controller builds its damping data from the named physical damper beams during initialization.
  - This avoids changing a curve on the beams but accidentally leaving the controller with an older copy of the damping values.
  - The additional high-speed beam is included when reporting the full compression curve, and the two transition velocities are treated as velocity 1 / velocity 2 rather than assuming that every valve design has the same “high-speed point”.
  - LRS release strength now belongs to the selected valving. Type-A and Type-B can use different rebound-relief factors on gravel, tarmac and RX.
  - The factor also follows the rebound adjuster through a **±0.05 range**: softer rebound uses less relief, while firmer rebound allows more relief when LRS opens.
  - Retuned the trigger thresholds and the mass / spring / damping arrangement used by the detection mechanism.
  - Added selectable per-wheel live debugging for opening / closing behaviour, so one corner can be observed without disabling the other axle in JBeam.
  - This remains an inertial mechanical detector in the model. It is not simply reading the wheel-load figure from a HUD and opening a valve at a chosen number of kilograms.

- **REBUILT THE ANTI-ROLL BARS AROUND ADJUSTABLE CANTILEVERS**
  - Replaced the old adjustment at the lower control-arm pickup with three effective cantilever-length positions: **L / M / H**.
  - Each axle still has three bar sizes. Bar diameter and cantilever position are separate choices, so the same bar has three useful stiffness settings.
  - The tuning variable now selects position 1, 2 or 3; the corresponding length change is calculated in the beams. It no longer exposes a raw precompression distance as the adjustment.
  - Added **solid and lightweight cantilever parts**. Their nodes and beams belong to those parts, so selecting a different cantilever also changes the corresponding weight.
  - Restored a triangulated lower pickup structure in the subframe, and made its positioning follow the control-arm mounting-height adjustment.
  - Removed the obsolete ARB adjustment beams from the suspension groups. The subframe, cantilever and bar parts now have clearer responsibilities.
  - Reworked the visual arrangement: the cantilevers use separate left / right props, the transverse bar has its own flexbody, and the drop-link meshes are retained. Changing the effective lever length no longer needs to squash the visible cantilever as a flexbody.
  - Moved the front and rear ARB controls, including their debug settings, into dedicated **Anti-Roll Bar** categories.

- **THE OLD ARB MEASUREMENT WAS NOT MEASURING WHAT I THOUGHT IT WAS**
  - I was measuring the vertical movement of the cantilever tip and using that to compare the bars. That works only under restricted geometry assumptions.
  - A tip moving farther in Z does not necessarily mean the bar has twisted farther. Different radii and starting angles can change the vertical displacement even when the actual rotation is the same.
  - The new workbook calculates angular movement around the bar's axis from the node positions, then compares it with wheel travel. This also accounts for the fact that the two reference nodes do not necessarily share the same X coordinate.
  - Measurements start from the **settled, fully extended position after precompression**, using the wheel jack. The original spawn coordinates are not a reliable start point for every cantilever setting.
  - The resulting equivalent wheel rate follows **torsional stiffness × angular motion ratio squared**, with the appropriate unit conversion. The geometry already accounts for the cantilever; its length must not be applied a second time to the torsion stiffness.
  - Front and rear bars now share one material / effective torsion-length baseline in the workbook, with the bar stiffness scaling by **diameter to the fourth power**.
  - The figures are an equivalent rate over the measured travel interval. They do not claim that the instantaneous wheel rate is constant from full droop to full compression.
  - Both the geometry and the measurement method have changed, so the larger new wheel-rate figures should not be read as a simple percentage increase over the old Z-only estimates.
  - The revised geometry gives the three positions a much more useful separation. Subsequent setup testing also showed that softening the rear ARB could improve gravel power-on turning, rather than requiring progressively more rear differential locking to compensate for an unloaded inside wheel.

- Added **Low / High control-arm mounting points**. The front adjustment changes the anti-dive geometry; the rear adjustment provides a suspension-geometry option.
- Reorganized these controls with camber, toe and caster under **Alignment**. Removed track-width tuning.
- Fixed the steering-rack height and replaced the rack-shim adjustment with a front **Bumpsteer Spacer**, acting at the outer steering connection like the rear adjustment.
- Retuned tarmac suspension hardpoints, including the rear upright / link positions and the front steering-arm position.
- Simplified power-steering tuning to a single strength adjustment, removing the separate high- and low-speed offset controls.

- **RECALIBRATED SPRING LENGTHS AND RIDE HEIGHT WITH THE NEW MASS AND GAS SUPPORT**
  - Reduced the relevant damper gas-pressure defaults to **125 PSI** and retested the spring / platform settings around the resulting support force.
  - Spring lengths were adjusted by in-game measurement so changing the spring-rate option does not unintentionally produce a different reference ride height.
  - The measurements were checked while rolling slowly in neutral, reducing the influence of the tyre's discrete node positions when the car is stopped.
  - Increased Rally2 tarmac spring rates by **1.5 N/mm** and revised the rear gravel rates and default spring selection.
  - Reworked spring free lengths and platform offsets together, including the Rally2 tarmac range near the bottom of the platform. The updated spring workbook records actual free length, rather than presenting the JBeam preload-length parameter as the whole spring's length.
  - Retained the separate helper-spring setup and documented it with each vehicle family.

- Separated wheel / rim parts from tyre definitions and reorganized the associated hub, flexbody and slot data. Updated rim choices and naming, including the Meridian Competition RC15A / RC18A range and separate 15-inch center caps.
- Adjusted rally tyre load sensitivity, tread, sliding behaviour and sidewall support, and updated some tyre-width / sidewall parameters. A complete RX tyre revision is still reserved for the next version.

- **MIGRATED RX1 AND RX3 TO THE UPDATED SUSPENSION SYSTEM**
  - Both now use the revised structure, node weights and stop conventions, together with the new RX valving and LRS layout.
  - RA88 and RA68 configurations default to **Type-A**, with compression and rebound settings reset to the new midpoints.
  - RA88's HRS is fixed and follows the gravel calibration. The mechanical stop settings and feature allocation are retained separately from the rally models where required.

## Misc

- **RECALIBRATED THE BRAKE SYSTEM AS A COMPLETE SET**
  - Revised the relationship between master-cylinder bore, pad multiplier, disc size, balance bar and maximum pedal force, instead of adjusting one coefficient in isolation.
  - The three main master cylinders are now **17.8 / 19.7 / 21.5 mm**, with hydraulic multipliers of **1.139 / 0.930 / 0.781**. Their relative effect follows inverse piston area.
  - The pad multipliers are now **A 0.72 / B 0.68 / C 0.65**, giving a meaningful difference between compounds while retaining A > B > C.
  - Rally2 and WRC17 tarmac configurations now use the **#1 front / #2 rear** cylinder combination. Gravel uses **#1 / #1**.
  - Recalibrated the disc constants so the tarmac braking-strength baseline is retained at the new **90% maximum pedal-force setting**. This refers to the tarmac baseline used during the brake work, not to an assumption that every configuration in 26.08.18 had the same pedal-force setting.
  - Preserved the relative strength of the different disc sizes, and adjusted the balance bar to retain the intended front / rear result. The revised primary tarmac setups use **59% balance bar**; this is not the same as 59% front wheel braking torque.
  - The balance-bar range is **35–65%**, with a **50% part default**. Saved configurations retain their own calibrated settings.
  - The handbrake calculation now includes its cylinder and lever factors and uses the same selected rear pad multiplier, rather than a separate hidden pad calibration.
  - Updated the brake-balance workbook to match the parts. It has a **3 × 3 front / rear master-cylinder matrix**, selectable hardware and a fixed percentage colour scale.
  - Brake thermodynamics are a separate task and are not part of this recalibration.

- **ADDED A CONSISTENT ENGLISH / SIMPLIFIED CHINESE LOCALIZATION SYSTEM**
  - Localized part names, slot names, tuning labels and descriptions, configuration names, configuration descriptions and categories.
  - The two dictionaries currently contain **417 matching entries each**, with reusable name templates for brands, models and prefixes.
  - Replaced full English sentences used as dictionary keys with short, consistent identifiers. Variable descriptions use the variable name, and configuration names / descriptions use a common vehiclesData hierarchy.
  - This also allows surface and event names to translate as part of the configuration name, instead of leaving half of a Chinese menu in English.
  - Corrected the event naming, including **Safari / Kenya** and the broader Nordic meaning of **Arctic**.
  - Corrected the distinction between the **PS values in BeamNG's power metadata** and **hp wording in configuration descriptions**.

- Reviewed the **16 main Rally2 / WRC17 gravel and tarmac configurations** individually: differential preload and ramps, ARB sizes and positions, low- / high-speed compression, rebound, brake settings, ballast and platform offsets.
- Retained separate Balanced, Arctic, Finland, Safari, Circuit, Croatia and Monte setups rather than replacing them all with the same defaults.
- Updated older derived configurations where shared parts or renamed variables required it, and removed obsolete saved tuning fields.
- Standardized the underlying WRC17 configuration, info and thumbnail filenames from **wrc+** to **wrc17**, while retaining the intended **WRC+ display name**.

- Renamed and regrouped the Rally1 configurations as **Rally1 2022 Prototype** and **Rally1 2025 Prototype** under **Prototype / Testing**.
  - The 2022 gravel prototype represents the 2022–2024 specification with hybrid-system ballast. A functioning hybrid boost / regeneration controller is **not implemented** yet.
  - The 2025 tarmac prototype represents the 2025–2026 specification without that hybrid ballast.
  - The hidden **Rally2 WRC Kit** is separately identified as a 2027 prototype, avoiding confusion with the older WRC specification.

  - Updated the ARB workbook to compare front and rear axles in separate gravel and tarmac charts, using the new angular-motion measurements and shared diameter-based stiffness baseline.
  - Updated the differential ramp calculator, brake-balance matrix and throttle-curve calculator to match the implemented parameters.
  - Added the spring / ride-height workbook with free lengths, rates, helper springs, spring colours and the recorded axle-weight / ride-height references.
  - Updated the suspension node / rail calculator and standardized workbook fonts, headers, alternating row colours and editable-input highlighting. Removed unnecessary explanatory text from the working tables.
